# BRAND.md — Chasing Fun

> The first file every weekly run reads.
> Defines who we are, who we're for, and what each newsletter is actually doing.
> Without this context, every output drifts toward generic surf-blog content.

---

## 1. The Company in One Line

**Chasing Fun is a surf-trip and community-experience brand** that helps Israelis live more — through the ocean, through travel, and through the kind of conversations you only have when you're salty, tired, and happy.

We are not "a tour operator." We are not "a wellness brand." We are not "a content creator."
We are a **community of people choosing aliveness**, organized around trips, content, and shared rituals.

---

## 2. The Founder Voice

The newsletter is written by **Dor Damari** in first person.
Not a faceless brand voice. Not a marketing team.
**Dor.**

This is critical. Every line of every newsletter must sound like something Dor said out loud, not something a copywriter polished. Anything that smells of "engagement copy" has to be cut.

This single fact — that the voice is one real human's voice — is what separates Chasing Fun from every other surf brand on the planet.

## 3. Who We're For (Audiences)

### Primary: "The Young-at-Heart Israeli"

| Trait | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | 21–45 (the *vibe* matters more than the age) |
| Location | Israel, often Tel Aviv area but not only |
| Identity markers | Loves the sea, freedom, life. Curious. Looking for meaning that isn't preachy. |
| Career | Often professional / self-employed / tech / business owner — *time-rich enough to travel, money-rich enough to invest in experiences* |
| What they want | Real experiences with real people. Not Instagram trips. |
| What they don't want | Corporate wellness BS. Aggressive marketing. Performative spirituality. |
| Pain | Feel that life moved too fast. Want to remember that they're alive. |
| Trigger to act | A Friday morning where they read something honest and remember the sea exists. |

### Secondary: "The Curious Beginner"

People who don't surf yet but feel pulled toward it. They follow Chasing Fun because the *content* moves them, not because they've signed up for anything. The newsletter's job for them is to keep the relationship warm until they're ready.

### Tertiary: "The Returning Tribe"

People who already came on a trip. The newsletter reminds them they're still part of the community even when they're back in their normal lives. They share the newsletter with friends — they're our most valuable distribution channel.

## 4. Core Values (in priority order)

1. **Honesty over polish.** A real, slightly imperfect sentence beats a perfect marketing line every time.
2. **Aliveness over performance.** We're not selling a lifestyle. We're inviting people into more life.
3. **Community over audience.** A reader is a person, not a "user." We talk *to* people, not *at* them.
4. **The sea as a teacher.** Surfing is a metaphor for how we want people to live: read the conditions, commit, fall, paddle back.
5. **Quiet confidence.** We don't shout. We don't sell hard. We trust that the right people will recognize what we're doing.
6. **Showing up weekly.** Consistency is the brand. The newsletter is not an option — it's a practice.

## 5. Brand Promise

> Every Friday morning, you'll get five waves of thought from someone who's actually living this — the sea, the trips, the conversations, the small things that change a week. No filler, no funnel, no "click here." Just enough to make Friday feel different.

## 6. What We Are Not

A clear list of what to avoid is often more useful than a long list of what to do:

- ❌ We are not a guru brand. No "10 lessons that will transform your life."
- ❌ We are not a wellness brand. No bath salts, no breathwork courses, no "manifest your trip."
- ❌ We are not a clickbait brand. No "you won't believe what happened on the wave."
- ❌ We are not generic surf content. The world has plenty of "5 tips for beginner surfers" articles.
- ❌ We are not a brand that talks at people. Every newsletter feels like a message from a friend, not from a list.
- ❌ We do not chase trending topics. The newsletter follows Dor's life, not the news cycle.

## 7. Strategic Role of the Newsletter

The weekly newsletter does five jobs at the same time. Every post should consciously serve all five:

| # | Job | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Warmth** — keep the community feeling close | Readers feel like they just had a coffee with Dor |
| 2 | **Traffic** — drive clicks to the website | The page gets opened on phones and read for >2 minutes |
| 3 | **Trip-warming** — make readers ready to consider a trip | Soft mention near the end, never aggressive |
| 4 | **Values** — embed Chasing Fun's worldview week by week | Quotes, observations, perspectives that compound over time |
| 5 | **Archive** — build a content library that becomes SEO/blog | Each post is a self-contained, well-structured piece |

If a post serves only 3 of the 5 jobs, it's still good. If it serves 0–2, it's a miss.

## 8. The Newsletter's North Star Metric

Not opens. Not clicks.
**Time on page.**

If readers spend 3+ minutes reading the page, the newsletter is doing its job. Everything else — UTM tracking, conversion to trips, growth — flows from that.

## 9. Reference Names & Touchstones

When the agent isn't sure what voice/feel to aim for, it can lean on these touchstones:

- **Tone reference:** the way close friends text each other on a Saturday morning — warm, real, no agenda.
- **Aesthetic reference:** dark gradients, ocean greens, calm typography. Never neon. Never hyper-corporate.
- **Pacing reference:** 5–8 minute reads. Long enough to feel substantial, short enough to finish before the coffee gets cold.
- **Cultural reference:** Hebrew with the natural English/sea-slang sprinkled in (גלישה, lineup, סופ"ש), the way real surfers in Israel actually talk.

## 11. Anchors to CORE (operational must-knows)

- **Vision (one line):** *Make people feel alive.*
- **Newsletter audience (21–45) is broader than the Trip ICP (21–30).** Don't over-pitch trips — keep CTAs soft. Only a subset of readers are trip-buyers.
- **When mentioning trips:** they are *structured, premium, group experiences with real progression*. Never frame them as cheap, passive, or generic.
- **Best-performing content type for this audience:** video (Instagram-native). Wave #5 is the highest-leverage section — never skip it.
